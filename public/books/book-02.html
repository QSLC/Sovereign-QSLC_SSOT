Sample — Chapter 1 Intelligence vs Information

A dashboard can contain a thousand numbers and still leave its operator unable to choose a next step. Information describes conditions. Operational intelligence relates those conditions to a decision, a constraint, and a consequence. The difference appears when a delivery team sees twelve late orders and must decide whether to add capacity, change promises, or investigate a broken status feed.

In an illustrative warehouse, the assistant first reports that overdue orders increased. A better investigation separates orders by promised date, actual dispatch evidence, and missing scans. Half the apparent backlog consists of completed deliveries whose confirmation never arrived. Adding drivers would address the wrong problem. The useful intelligence is a distinction between service failure and reporting failure.

An intelligence layer should make its reasoning inspectable at the level needed for the decision. It should identify the question, relevant evidence, assumptions, available options, and uncertainties. This does not require exposing hidden internal model processes. It requires a clear operational explanation that another person can check against the records.

Begin with a decision brief rather than an all-purpose summary. State the decision and its deadline. Explain which facts affect it and which facts are merely interesting. Compare the available options against explicit criteria. If the data cannot distinguish between two options, say what additional observation would resolve the uncertainty and whether obtaining it is worth the delay.

A useful test is counterfactual: what would change this recommendation? If the assistant cannot name a condition that would alter its advice, it may be generating a persuasive narrative rather than a decision aid. In the warehouse, verified delivery confirmations would reduce the apparent backlog; verified undelivered orders would justify a capacity response. The recommendation has a testable hinge.

Take one metric you review regularly and write the decision it is supposed to inform. Then identify three different causes that could produce the same value. Ask what evidence distinguishes those causes. The result is a small intelligence specification. You have moved from asking the machine to describe a chart to asking it to help resolve a real uncertainty.
