Expanded 2026 Edition

Expanded Chapter 8 — The Public Product Boundary Is Part of the Product

The public QSLC EVE site now makes a valuable distinction visible: a product can be interactive without exposing private operations. Its public experience lists offers, teaches evidence governance, provides a synthetic living-calculator demo, and shows system topology while explicitly excluding private SSOT, payroll, banking, credentials, owner evidence, and administrative actions.

This boundary should be treated as a feature, not a limitation. Enterprise buyers do not need the founder’s bank balance to understand the governance engine. They need to see how records move, how approvals are blocked, how roles are separated, and how outputs are audited. The public surface should therefore sell the behavior of the system rather than the founder’s private numbers.

The same rule applies to books. Public examples should use synthetic or clearly labeled historical data. Private operating metrics stay in the controlled evidence vault. That separation increases both security and credibility.

Expanded Chapter 9 — Pricing the Knowledge Stack

A book series should not be priced by dividing an internal IP valuation by the number of pages. Readers pay for usefulness, clarity, specialization, format, and alternatives in the market. In October 2026, mainstream AI and productivity books commonly list around the high teens to low thirties in print, while specialized technical AI books can run much higher. That gives this series room for a stepped ladder: accessible founder narrative at the low end, field guides in the middle, and the formula/operations volumes at a premium.

The price ladder also mirrors depth. A reader can enter through Book 1 without buying the whole system. Each volume then resolves one question and opens the next. The Living Calculator costs more because it contains the dual registry, lineage rules, and technical reconciliation. The Operations Manual costs more because it converts the concepts into reusable governance and commercial design.

This is not artificial scarcity. Each book should stand on its own. The series effect comes from cumulative context. The reader who buys all volumes receives a complete path from founder story to system design, model lineage, operational governance, and one-person leverage.

Expanded Chapter 10 — Evidence as a Commercial Deliverable

Most service businesses sell an outcome and then struggle to prove how they produced it. An evidence-first system can sell the outcome and the proof package together. A completed workflow can include source references, approvals, timestamps, test results, exceptions, and final state.

That proof package can become a differentiator. In logistics, it can show what was dispatched and why. In payroll, it can show which hours and rates produced a submission. In software, it can show which build, tests, and approvals produced a release. In content, it can show which source files produced a published edition.
